## 🏛️ MODULE 1: Computing Foundations, History, & Digital Citizenship

### 📐 1.1 The Nature of a Computer

- **Computer**
    
    - ⤷ **Definition**: An electronic/mechanical machine that accepts raw data, manipulates it via programmed rules, yields actionable results, stores output for future retrieval, and operates under software control.
        
    - ↳ **The "Digital vs. Computer" Rule**: ==Possessing a digital display (e.g., a basic digital wristwatch) does NOT make a device a full computer== unless it satisfies all five determinative criteria. Conversely, ancient manual tools like the **abacus** qualify as early mechanical computers because they systematically compute data.
        

> [!check] 🔢 High-Yield Enumeration: 5 Determinative Criteria of a Computer
> 
> 1. **Input** (Accepting raw data)
>     
> 2. **Processing** (Manipulating data according to rules)
>     
> 3. **Output** (Producing usable results)
>     
> 4. **Storage** (Retaining results for future reference)
>     
> 5. **Programmability** (Operating under modifiable software control)
>     

### 🕰️ 1.2 Chronological Lineage of Computing

- **Ancient Mechanical Era**
    
    - ⤷ **Sumerian Abacus ($2700\text{--}2300\text{ BC}$)**: Earliest documented manual calculating device.
        
    - ⤷ **Chinese Abacus ($1050\text{--}771\text{ BC}$)**: Bead-and-rod mechanical arithmetic frame.
        
    - ⤷ **Antikythera Mechanism (Greece, $\sim 100\text{ BC}$)**: Early analogue geared astronomical computer used to predict celestial positions.
        
- **19th-Century Mechanical & Programmable Pioneers**
    
    - ⤷ **Analytical Engine ($1837$)**: Designed by **Charles Babbage** alongside **Ada Lovelace**; established the first general-purpose programmable computer design.
        
    - ⤷ **Arithmometer ($1887$)**: Built by **Thomas de Colmar / Payen**; the first mass-produced commercial mechanical calculator.
        
- **20th-Century Electronic & Generational Shifts**
    
    - ⤷ **ENIAC ($1945$)**: _Electronic Numerical Integrator and Computer_; ==first operational general-purpose electronic computer== (built using vacuum tubes).
        
    - ⤷ **UNIVAC ($1951$)**: _Universal Automatic Computer_; ==first commercially produced general-purpose computer==.
        
    - ⤷ **1950s Hardware Shift**: Transition from vacuum tubes to **Transistors**.
        
    - ⤷ **1960s Hardware Shift**: Adoption of **Integrated Circuits (IC)**.
        
    - ⤷ **1970s Hardware Shift**: Invention of **Microprocessors**.
        
    - ⤷ **Apple II ($1978$)**: Landmark commercial microcomputer engineered by **Steve Jobs & Steve Wozniak**.
        
    - ⤷ **Future Horizon**: **Quantum Computers**—utilise quantum bits (**qubits**) to process states simultaneously (e.g., Google's _Sycamore_).

- **Moore's Law**
    
    - ⤷ **Core Rule**: Formulated by Intel co-founder **Gordon Moore**; states that the ==number of transistors on a microchip doubles roughly every two years==.
        
    - ↳ **Epistemic Status**: ==A historical technological and business observation/trend, NOT an immutable law of physics==.
        

> [!warning] ⚠️ Exam Trap & Edge Case: ENIAC vs. UNIVAC & Moore's Law Examiners routinely swap **ENIAC ($1945$)** and **UNIVAC ($1951$)**. Remember: **E**NIAC came first as the **E**xperimental/operational electronic pioneer; **U**NIVAC came second for **U**niversal commercial sale. Furthermore, if a true/false item claims Moore's Law is a physical law of thermodynamics or electromagnetism, mark it **False**—it is an economic and engineering cadence.

### 🧠 2.2 Processor (CPU) Architecture & The Machine Cycle

- **Central Processing Unit (CPU)**
    
    - ⤷ **Definition**: The microchip that interprets and executes basic operating instructions.
        

> [!check] 🔢 High-Yield Enumeration: 2 Main Functional Units of a Processor (CPU)
> 
> 1. **Control Unit (CU)**: Directs and coordinates all hardware operations and governs machine cycle traffic.
>     
> 2. **Arithmetic Logic Unit (ALU)**: Executes mathematical calculations (addition, subtraction) and logical evaluations (boolean comparisons such as $\text{AND}$, $\text{OR}$, $\text{NOT}$).
>     

> [!example] 🧪 Step-by-Step Derivation: The 4-Step Machine Cycle Pipeline
> 
> $$\text{Fetch (from RAM by CU)} \longrightarrow \text{Decode (by CU to ALU)} \longrightarrow \text{Execute (by ALU)} \longrightarrow \text{Store (to RAM)}$$
> 
> 1. **Step 1: Fetch** — The **Control Unit (CU)** retrieves the next instruction and required data from **RAM**.
>     
> 2. **Step 2: Decode** — The **CU** translates the instruction into electronic signals and routes the data to the **ALU**.
>     
> 3. **Step 3: Execute** — The **ALU** performs the actual arithmetic calculation or logical test.
>     
> 4. **Step 4: Store** — The resulting output is written back into **RAM** for use or display.
>     

> [!tip] 💡 Intuition: The Kitchen Brigade Analogy for the CPU
> 
> Picture a high-end restaurant kitchen. **RAM** is the countertop holding raw ingredients. The **Control Unit (CU)** is the Head Expediter who grabs the ticket from the counter (**Fetch**), reads the order aloud and preps the station (**Decode**), and hands it to the Line Chef. The **Arithmetic Logic Unit (ALU)** is the Line Chef who actually chops, sears, and seasons the food (**Execute**), before placing the finished plate back onto the pass counter (**Store**).

### 📶 2.3 Signals & Binary Data Hierarchy

- **Signal Representation**
    
    - ⤷ **Analogue Signals**: Continuous wave signals that fluctuate smoothly in amplitude and quality (e.g., human speech, vinyl records, mercury thermometers).
        
    - ⤷ **Digital Signals**: Discrete, two-state binary impulses ($0$ and $1$).
        
        - ↳ $\text{Bit } 1 = \text{Electronic Charge ON}$
            
        - ↳ $\text{Bit } 0 = \text{Electronic Charge OFF}$
            
- **Memory Data Storage Hierarchy**
    
    - ⤷ **Fundamental Ratio**:
        
        $$1\text{ Byte} = 8\text{ Bits}$$
        
    - ⤷ **Scaling Progression (Binary Multiples of $2^{10} = 1,024$)**:
        

|**Unit Name**|**Exact Byte Equivalent**|**Real-World Text Capacity Equivalent**|
|---|---|---|
|**Bit ($\text{b}$)**|$\frac{1}{8}\text{ Byte}$|Single binary state ($0$ or $1$)|
|**Byte ($\text{B}$)**|$8\text{ Bits}$|Single character of text|
|**Kilobyte ($\text{KB}$)**|$1,024\text{ Bytes}$|$\approx \frac{1}{2}\text{ page of text}$|
|**Megabyte ($\text{MB}$)**|$1,048,576\text{ Bytes}$|$\approx 500\text{ pages of text}$|
|**Gigabyte ($\text{GB}$)**|$1,073,741,824\text{ Bytes}$|$\approx 500,000\text{ pages of text}$|
|**Terabyte ($\text{TB}$)**|$1,099,511,627,776\text{ Bytes}$|$\approx 500,000,000\text{ pages of text}$|
|**Higher Tiers (Ascending)**|$\text{PB} \rightarrow \text{EB} \rightarrow \text{ZB} \rightarrow \text{YB}$|**Petabyte (PB)** $\rightarrow$ **Exabyte (EB)** $\rightarrow$ **Zettabyte (ZB)** $\rightarrow$ **Yottabyte (YB)**|

### 💾 2.4 Primary Memory Systems (RAM, ROM, & Flash)

|**Feature**|**Volatile Memory (RAM)**|**Nonvolatile Memory (ROM / Flash)**|
|---|---|---|
|**Power Dependency**|==Loses all data when electrical power is switched off==|==Retains data permanently without electrical power==|
|**Primary Examples**|Main system RAM (**DRAM**, **SRAM**)|**BIOS ROM**, **Solid-State Drives (SSD)**, **USB Flash**|
|**System Role**|Holds active programs and temporary workspace|Stores startup bootstrap code, firmware, OS files|

> [!tip] 💡 Intuition: Why SRAM is Cache and DRAM is Main Memory Think of **DRAM** as a leaky bucket: it is cheap to manufacture in large quantities ($8\text{ GB}$ to $32\text{ GB}$), but you have to keep pouring electricity into it thousands of times a second or the water (data) leaks out. **SRAM** is a sealed steel tank: it doesn't leak and delivers water instantaneously without needing a refill pump, making it ideal for the CPU's ultra-fast internal cache, though far too expensive to use for the whole computer's main memory.

### 🔄 3.3 OS Resource Management & Utility Tools

- **Virtual Memory Mechanics**
    
    - ⤷ **Core Rule**: Co-opts secondary storage (hard drive/SSD) space to act as an overflow extension when physical RAM fills up.
        
    - ↳ **Page Out**: When RAM is exhausted, the OS moves least-recently-used data blocks from RAM into a temporary **swap file** on the storage drive.
        
    - ↳ **Page In**: When the CPU needs those swapped data blocks again, the OS reads them from the disk swap file back into active **RAM**.
        
- **Print Spooling**
    
    - ⤷ **Core Rule**: The OS intercepts print jobs and writes them into a temporary buffer queue (**spooler**) on the disk/memory.
        
    - ↳ **Why It Matters**: Frees up the originating application immediately so the user can keep working while the mechanical printer outputs pages at its own slow speed.
        
- **Device Drivers & Plug and Play (PnP)**
    
    - ⤷ **Driver**: A specialised translator program that teaches the OS how to communicate with a specific hardware peripheral.
        
    - ⤷ **Plug and Play (PnP)**: The OS capability to automatically detect, configure, and install driver support for newly attached hardware.

> [!check] 🔢 High-Yield Enumeration: 4 Physical Network Topologies
> 
> 8. **Star Topology**: All nodes connect to a central hub, switch, or router.
>     
>     - ↳ _Advantage_: Easy to manage and add devices.
>         
>     - ↳ ❌ _Single Point of Failure_: ==If the central switch fails, the entire network collapses==.
>         
> 9. **Bus Topology**: All nodes tap into a single shared central backbone cable.
>     
>     - ↳ _Advantage_: Low cabling cost.
>         
>     - ↳ ❌ _Single Point of Failure_: ==A break anywhere along the main backbone cable terminates all communications==.
>         
> 10. **Ring Topology**: Nodes link in a continuous closed loop; packets travel node-to-node in sequence.
>     
>     - ↳ _Advantage_: Orderly, predictable data flow.
>         
>     - ↳ ❌ _Single Point of Failure_: ==A single broken link or dead node can sever the entire loop==.
>         
> 11. **Mesh Topology**: Every node maintains redundant interconnections with other nodes (Full vs. Partial Mesh).
>     
>     - ↳ _Advantage_: ==Unmatched fault tolerance== (traffic automatically reroutes around severed lines).
>         
>     - ↳ ❌ _Drawback_: Expensive and complex to wire and configure.
>         

- **Key Network Standards & Protocols**
    
    - ⤷ **Ethernet**: Local network standard where ==no single computer controls line transmission==; relies on **collision detection**.
        
    - ⤷ **Token Ring**: Passes a special electronic authorisation packet (**"token"**) around a logical ring; ==only the node holding the token is permitted to transmit== (eliminating collisions).
        
    - ⤷ **TCP/IP**: The foundational protocol suite of the Internet; governs how data is sliced into packets, addressed, and routed.
        
    - ⤷ **Wi-Fi ($802.11$)**: Standard wireless Ethernet local transmission protocol.
        
    - ⤷ **WiMAX ($802.16$)**: Long-range broadband wireless metropolitan protocol with towers covering up to a **==$30\text{-mile radius}$==**.
        
    - ⤷ **WAP (Wireless Application Protocol)**: Standard specifying how mobile handheld devices render internet, web, and email content.

## 🛡️ MODULE 5: Digital Security, Attack Vectors, & Netiquette

### 🕵️ 5.1 Threat Actors & Cybercriminals

> [!check] 🔢 High-Yield Enumeration: 6 Types of Cybercriminals / Threat Actors
> 
> 1. **Script Kiddies**: Unskilled intruders with minimal technical comprehension who deploy pre-written scripts and automated exploit tools authored by others.
>     
> 2. **Hackers**: Threat actors who access computer systems or networks **without authorisation**.
>     
> 3. **Crackers**: Malicious intruders who breach systems illegally with the **explicit intent to destroy files, steal data, or cause harm**.
>     
> 4. **Hacktivists**: Ideologically driven actors who infiltrate networks to advance **political, social, or philosophical causes**.
>     
> 5. **Cyberterrorists**: Operatives targeting **critical national infrastructure** (electrical grids, air traffic control) to incite mass panic and threaten national security.
>     
> 6. **Cyberextortionists**: Criminals who demand financial **ransom** under threat of leaking confidential corporate data or launching crippling attacks.
>     

### ⚔️ 5.2 Primary Network Attack Vectors & Safeguards

> [!check] 🔢 High-Yield Enumeration: 6 Primary Network / Internet Attack Vectors
> 
> 1. **Malware**: Any maliciously engineered software designed to infiltrate or damage a computer system.
>     
>     - → _Historical Case_: **WannaCry ($2017$)** ransomware/worm.
>         
> 2. **Botnets (Zombie Army)**: A legion of hijacked computers/IoT devices (**zombies**) secretly controlled by a remote **botmaster** to launch DDoS floods, spam campaigns, or crypto-mining.
>     
>     - → _Historical Case_: **Mirai ($2016$)** botnet enslaving IoT webcams and routers.
>         
> 3. **Denial of Service (DoS / DDoS)**: Flooding a target server with bogus traffic requests until its CPU/bandwidth is exhausted, locking out legitimate users.
>     
>     - → _Historical Case_: **Dyn DNS Attack ($2016$)**.
>         
> 4. **Backdoors**: A hidden software routine or configuration portal that bypasses normal authentication to grant persistent administrative entry.
>     
>     - → _Historical Case_: **SolarWinds ($2020$)** supply-chain breach.
>         
> 5. **Spoofing**: Forging packet source addresses or website domain headers so an attacker masquerades as a trusted machine or institution.
>     
> 6. **Social Engineering**: Manipulating human psychology, fear, or trust to trick victims into surrendering passwords or sensitive data (primary variant: **Phishing**).
>     

> [!check] 🔢 High-Yield Enumeration: 5 Information Privacy & Authentication Safeguards
> 
> 1. **Strong Passwords**
>     
> 2. **Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)**
>     
> 3. **Biometric Devices** (Fingerprint, iris, facial recognition)
>     
> 4. **CAPTCHA** (_Completely Automated Public Turing test to tell Computers and Humans Apart_)
>     
> 5. **Encryption**
>     

### 🤝 5.3 Netiquette (Network Etiquette)

- **The Golden Rule of Netiquette**
    
    - ⤷ **Core Maxim**: ==Treat others online exactly as you would like them to treat you==.
        
- **Behavioural & Stylistic Mandates**
    
    - ⤷ **Orthography & Tone**: Keep emails concise, employ proper grammar, and ==never type in **ALL CAPITAL LETTERS**== (universally interpreted as SHOUTING).
        
    - ⤷ **Conflict Avoidance**: Refrain from posting **flames** (hostile insults or provocative attacks) and refuse to participate in **flame wars**.
        
    - ⤷ **Privacy Respect**: Never read another individual's private email correspondence.
        
    - ⤷ **Forum Preparation**: Always consult a community's **FAQ (Frequently Asked Questions)** document before posting basic, repetitive queries.
