## 🏛️ PART 2: CORE CONCEPTS BY MODULE

### MODULE 1: Computing Systems, History, & Digital Citizenship

- **Computer**: A machine that accepts raw data (input), manipulates it according to programmed rules (processing), yields results (output), stores results for future reference (storage), and operates under software control (programmability).
    
- **The "Digital vs. Computer" Rule**: Having a digital display (e.g., a digital watch) does not automatically make an item a full computer unless it performs input, programmable processing, storage, and output. Conversely, ancient manual calculating tools like the **abacus** qualify as early mechanical computers because they systematically compute data.
    
- **Historical Highlights**:
    
    - _Ancient_: Sumerian Abacus (2700–2300 BC), Chinese Abacus (1050–771 BC), Antikythera Mechanism (Greece, ~100 BC – early analog geared astronomical computer).
        
    - _19th Century_: Charles Babbage & Ada Lovelace designed the **Analytical Engine** (1837; general-purpose programmable design). Thomas de Colmar / Payen built the **Arithmometer** (1887; first mass-produced commercial mechanical calculator).
        
    - _20th Century Electronic Machines_:
        
        - **ENIAC (1945)**: First operational general-purpose electronic computer (vacuum-tube based).
            
        - **UNIVAC (1951)**: First commercially produced general-purpose computer.
            
        - _Generational Hardware Shifts_: 1950s = Transistors; 1960s = Integrated Circuits (IC); 1970s = Microprocessors; 1978 = Apple II (Jobs & Wozniak microcomputer).
            
        - _Future Horizon_: Quantum Computers (using qubits; e.g., Google's _Sycamore_).
            
- **Moore's Law**: Formulated by Intel co-founder Gordon Moore. States that the **number of transistors on a microchip doubles roughly every two years**. It is a historical technological and business trend, not an immutable law of physics.
    
- **Digital Literacy**: The ability to effectively and safely use digital tools, evaluate online information critically, and maintain ethical behavior.
    
- **Digital Citizen**: An individual who navigates, communicates, and participates ethically, legally, and responsibly within digital environments.
    
- **The Digital Divide**: The economic and social inequality between communities that have access to modern information and communication technology and those that do not.
    
- **Cybersecurity vs. Cybercrime**:
    
    - _Cybersecurity_: The practice of protecting computers, servers, networks, and data from malicious digital attacks and unauthorized exposure.
        
    - _Cybercrime_: Any illegal act involving a computer, network, or electronic device.
        
    - _Data Privacy_: The legal and ethical right of individuals to determine what data about them is collected, how it is used, and with whom it is shared.
        
    - _RA 10175_: The **Cybercrime Prevention Act of 2012** (Philippines law defining offenses, penalties, and enforcement mechanisms for cybercrimes).
        

### MODULE 2: Hardware Architecture, CPU, & Memory Systems

- **The System Unit**: The external chassis/case housing the internal electronic components used to process computer data.
    
- **Motherboard**: The main circuit board of the system unit to which all components connect.
    
- **Processor (CPU)**: Interprets and executes basic operating instructions:
    
    - _Control Unit (CU)_: Directs and coordinates all hardware operations and controls the cycle flow.
        
    - _Arithmetic Logic Unit (ALU)_: Performs mathematical operations (addition, subtraction) and logical evaluations (comparisons like AND, OR, NOT).
        
- **The Machine Cycle Pipeline**:  
    
    $$\text{Fetch (from RAM by CU)} \longrightarrow \text{Decode (by CU to ALU)} \longrightarrow \text{Execute (by ALU)} \longrightarrow \text{Store (to RAM)}$$
- **Signals & Data Representation**:
    
    - _Analog Signals_: Continuous wave signals varying in strength and quality (e.g., human voice, vinyl records, mercury thermometers).
        
    - _Digital Signals_: Discrete, binary states ($0$ and $1$). Bit $1 = \text{Electronic Charge ON}$; Bit $0 = \text{Electronic Charge OFF}$.
        
- **Memory Data Storage Hierarchy**:
    
    - $1 \text{ Byte} = 8 \text{ Bits}$.
        
    - $1 \text{ Kilobyte (KB)} = 1,024 \text{ Bytes}$ ($\approx \frac{1}{2}$ page of text).
        
    - $1 \text{ Megabyte (MB)} = 1,048,576 \text{ Bytes}$ ($\approx 500$ pages of text).
        
    - $1 \text{ Gigabyte (GB)} = 1,073,741,824 \text{ Bytes}$ ($\approx 500,000$ pages of text).
        
    - $1 \text{ Terabyte (TB)} = 1,099,511,627,776 \text{ Bytes}$ ($\approx 500,000,000$ pages of text).
        
    - Ascending order beyond TB: **Petabyte (PB)** $\rightarrow$ **Exabyte (EB)** $\rightarrow$ **Zettabyte (ZB)** $\rightarrow$ **Yottabyte (YB)**.
        
- **Volatile vs. Nonvolatile Memory**:
    
    - _Volatile Memory_: Erases contents when power is switched off (e.g., **RAM**).
        
    - _Nonvolatile Memory_: Permanently retains instructions even without electrical power (e.g., **ROM**, **Flash memory**).
        
- **RAM Types & Characteristics**:
    
    - _DRAM (Dynamic RAM)_: Must be refreshed dynamically with electricity thousands of times per second.
        
    - _SRAM (Static RAM)_: Faster, more reliable, holds data without constant refreshing (commonly used in CPU cache).
        
    - _MRAM (Magnetoresistive RAM)_: Stores data using magnetic charges instead of electrical charges.
        
- **ROM & Flash**:
    
    - _ROM (Read-Only Memory)_: Hard-coded, permanent factory instructions (firmware).
        
    - _PROM (Programmable ROM)_: Blank memory chip written to once by a hardware developer; cannot be altered thereafter.
        
    - _Flash Memory_: Electronically erasable, rewritable nonvolatile solid-state memory (used in USB flash drives and SSDs).
        
- **Specialized Input Scanners**:
    
    - _RFID (Radio Frequency Identification)_: Uses radio frequency waves to read data embedded in wireless transponder tags.
        
    - _OMR (Optical Mark Recognition)_: Detects hand-drawn pencil/pen bubbles on test scoring sheets.
        
    - _MICR (Magnetic Ink Character Recognition)_: Reads characters encoded with magnetized ink at the bottom of bank checks.
        

### MODULE 3: Software Systems & Operating Systems

- **Application Software Forms**:
    
    - _Packaged Software_: Standardized, off-the-shelf software distributed for commercial mass sale (e.g., Microsoft Office).
        
    - _Custom Software_: Tailor-made software engineered exclusively for a specific organization's unique requirements.
        
    - _Web Application_: Software hosted on remote servers accessible through any web browser without local installation (e.g., Google Docs, Canva).
        
    - _Open-Source Software (OSS)_: Software provided with completely accessible, modifiable, and redistributable source code (e.g., Linux, Mozilla Firefox, Python).
        
    - _Shareware_: Copyrighted software provided free for an evaluation trial period, after which payment is required.
        
    - _Freeware_: Copyrighted software supplied at no cost for permanent personal use, but whose underlying source code is not provided.
        
    - _Public Domain Software_: Software with no copyright restrictions donated entirely to the public; free to alter and distribute without attribution.
        
- **Operating System (OS)**: A coordinated collection of system programs that manages computer hardware resources and acts as an intermediary between hardware and user applications.
    
- **Power States**:
    
    - _Sleep Mode_: Copies open sessions to **RAM**, powers down non-essential hardware, and places the machine in an ultra-low-power state for rapid resume.
        
    - _Hibernate Mode_: Saves all active programs and system states directly to the **hard drive/SSD**, then completely powers the machine off.
        
- **User Interface Models**:
    
    - _GUI (Graphical User Interface)_: Intuitive interface where users interact with icons, buttons, menus, and visual windows.
        
    - _CLI (Command-Line Interface)_: Text-based console where users must type precise alphanumeric commands, keywords, and arguments.
        
- **Virtual Memory Mechanics**:
    
    - Uses secondary storage (hard drive/SSD) space as an overflow extension for physical RAM.
        
    - _Paging / Swap File_: When physical RAM fills up, the OS executes a **Page Out**, moving least-recently-used data blocks into a temporary _swap file_ on the disk. When needed again, the OS executes a **Page In**, reading data back into active RAM.
        
- **Print Spooling**: The OS transfers print tasks into a buffer queue (**spooler**) on the disk/memory. This frees up the originating software application immediately so the user can continue working while the printer prints at its own pace.
    
- **Device Drivers & Plug and Play (PnP)**:
    
    - _Driver_: A specialized software routine that teaches the OS how to communicate with a specific peripheral.
        
    - _Plug and Play (PnP)_: The built-in capability of an OS to automatically recognize, configure, and install driver support for newly connected devices.
        

### MODULE 4: The Internet, The Web, & Computer Communications

- **Internet vs. World Wide Web**:
    
    - The _Internet_ is the physical, global interconnection of computer networks.
        
    - The _World Wide Web (WWW)_ is an information-sharing service operating over the Internet, consisting of interlinked hypertext documents (Web pages).
        
- **Important Internet Milestones**:
    
    - _September 1969_: ARPANET goes live with 4 university nodes.
        
    - _1971_: Ray Tomlinson invents network email and introduces the `@` separator.
        
    - _January 1, 1983_: **Official Birthday of the Internet**; standard adoption of **TCP/IP**.
        
    - _1989–1991_: Tim Berners-Lee invents HTML, HTTP, URLs, and the World Wide Web at CERN.
        
    - _1993_: Release of NCSA Mosaic, the first graphical web browser.
        
- **IP Address & Domain Structure**:
    
    - _IP Address_: A unique numerical label (e.g., `72.14.207.99`).
        
    - _Domain Name_: The human-friendly text alias (e.g., `www.google.com`).
        
    - _DNS Server_: Resolves and translates textual domain names into numeric IP addresses.
        
- **Search Tools**:
    
    - _Search Engine_: Software that crawls and indexes web pages, locating documents matching specific user keywords (e.g., Google, Bing).
        
    - _Subject Directory_: A categorized, hierarchically structured collection of websites reviewed, organized, and curated by human editors.
        
- **Intranet vs. Extranet**:
    
    - _Intranet_: An exclusive, internal private corporate network that uses standard internet and web technologies for internal employees.
        
    - _Extranet_: A secure portal extending selected segments of an organization's internal intranet out to authenticated external parties (suppliers, contractors, corporate clients, franchisees).
        
- **Network Topologies**:
    
    - _Star Topology_: All devices connect to a central hub, switch, or router. Easy to manage and expand. _Vulnerability_: Central switch failure halts the entire network.
        
    - _Bus Topology_: Devices connect to a single central backbone transmission cable. Low cost. _Vulnerability_: A severed main line terminates all communications.
        
    - _Ring Topology_: Devices link in a continuous closed loop; tokens or packets pass from node to node. Orderly data flow. _Vulnerability_: A break in one device or link can break the loop.
        
    - _Mesh Topology_: Devices maintain redundant links to other nodes (Full vs. Partial Mesh). _Advantage_: Unmatched fault tolerance (data reroutes if a line breaks). _Vulnerability_: Expensive to wire and configure.
        
- **Key Network Standards & Protocols**:
    
    - _Ethernet_: Local network standard stating that no single computer controls when lines transmit; uses collision detection.
        
    - _Token Ring_: Passes an electronic authorization packet ("token") around a logical ring; only the station holding the token can transmit.
        
    - _TCP/IP_: The core communication protocol suite of the Internet; defines packet assembly and routing.
        
    - _Wi-Fi (802.11)_: Wireless Ethernet transmission standard.
        
    - _WiMAX (802.16)_: Long-range broadband wireless protocol with towers capable of covering a **30-mile radius**.
        
    - _WAP (Wireless Application Protocol)_: Specifies how mobile handheld terminals display internet, web, and email content.
        

### MODULE 5: Digital Security, Attack Vectors, & Netiquette

- **Categories of Cybercriminals**:
    
    - _Script Kiddies_: Attackers with minimal technical understanding who execute pre-written scripts and automated exploit tools authored by others.
        
    - _Hacker_: A threat actor who accesses computer systems without authorization.
        
    - _Cracker_: An intruder who accesses networks illegally with explicit malicious intent to destroy files, commit theft, or cause harm.
        
    - _Hacktivist_: An individual who infiltrates systems to advance ideological, political, or social causes.
        
    - _Cyberterrorist_: Threat actor targeting public infrastructure or critical systems (e.g., electrical grids, air traffic systems) to induce mass panic and compromise national security.
        
    - _Cyberextortionist_: An extortionist who demands financial ransom under threat of leaking confidential corporate files or deploying disabling attacks.
        
- **Network & Security Attacks**:
    
    - _Malware_: Any malicious software designed to compromise or damage a system (e.g., _WannaCry_ 2017 ransomware/worm).
        
    - _Botnets_: A network of hijacked computers/devices (**zombies**) commanded remotely by a botmaster to execute DDoS floods, mass spam, or illicit cryptocurrency mining (e.g., _Mirai_ 2016 botnet targeting IoT webcams/routers).
        
    - _Denial of Service (DoS / DDoS)_: Flooding a target server with bogus connection requests to exhaust system resources, rendering services inaccessible to legitimate users (e.g., _Dyn_ DNS attack 2016).
        
    - _Backdoor_: A hidden software routine or configuration setting that bypasses standard authentication controls to grant persistent administrative access (e.g., _SolarWinds_ 2020 supply chain breach).
        
    - _Spoofing_: Transmitting packets with forged source addresses or spoofing website domain headers to deceive a target system into treating an intruder as a trusted entity.
        
    - _Social Engineering_: Manipulating human psychology and exploiting trust to trick people into divulging passwords or confidential data (primary variant: **Phishing**).
        
- **Netiquette Rules**:
    
    - **The Golden Rule**: Treat others online as you would like them to treat you.
        
    - _Writing_: Keep emails brief, use correct grammar, and do not use **ALL CAPITAL LETTERS** (which is perceived as SHOUTING).
        
    - _Conflict_: Avoid posting **flames** (insults/harassing messages) and do not engage in **flame wars**.
        
    - _Respect_: Never read another individual's private email messages.
        
    - _Preparation_: Check a forum's **FAQ (Frequently Asked Questions)** list before asking basic questions.
